The failed request had one attempt and no PostgreSQL error code. At response time its outcome was uncertain. The same writer backend was observed active in COMMIT **731.60 ms after the 503**, at least 3,748.84 ms after sending COMMIT. After settlement the database contained one receipt, two journals, 1,000 postings and one audit. Two fresh authorized replays both returned 200 with identical bodies. By contrast all three exhausted SSI requests had acknowledged rollbacks on all attempts and zero scoped financial rows before recovery. Their first same-key recovery created one operation (201), followed by an identical replay (200).

The local writer sets a 2 s statement timeout, 1.5 s lock timeout and remaining transaction budget (9,965 and 9,975 ms in sampled attempts). The pool configures a separate 3 s client query timer. The locked pg driver reports a read timeout to the caller and discards a late callback. The runner destroys the uncertain connection without an automatic COMMIT retry. PostgreSQL 17.6 disables its statement timer before finishing the transaction command, which is where deferred triggers run. The setting is present, but it cannot be interpreted as a two-second ceiling on this deferred work. Lock timeout limits individual lock waits. Neither the ten-second application deadline nor the transaction setting extends the client's three-second wait. The experiment did not test outer transaction-timeout enforcement. [PostgreSQL command completion](https://github.com/postgres/postgres/blob/REL_17_6/src/backend/tcop/postgres.c) · [PostgreSQL deferred trigger execution](https://github.com/postgres/postgres/blob/REL_17_6/src/backend/access/transam/xact.c)

**Likely dominant cost: repeated full-journal validation.** Each inserted leg updates the draft revision. Migration 004 queues one deferred journal check for that update and one for the posting, plus the initial journal insert and final seal. Two 500-leg children therefore schedule 2,004 journal assertion events, in addition to operation/audit/FK work. The assertion sums all legs, locks all affected accounts in order, checks the operation, and for reversals compares both posting multisets with EXCEPT ALL. In a separate rolled-back SERIALIZABLE probe running as the writer on durable maximum journals, 20 calls of the unchanged assertion took 20.138 ms for GENERAL and 31.912 ms for REVERSAL. Multiplying warm per-call means by 1,002 per child gives about 2.61 s, consistent with observed COMMIT timing. This estimate samples a warm standalone plan on posted rows. It is not an instrumented per-trigger timing inside COMMIT. [Migration 004, assertions and triggers](../../prisma/migrations/202611110004_ledger_schema/migration.sql)

The 111 activity samples contained no reported wait event. Scheduler delay, cache state, WAL/fsync and short unsampled waits remain possible contributors. Function tracking was off. No server profiler or CI host instrumentation was enabled. Both publication CI jobs independently reported client timeouts at 3,000.76 / 3,001.10 ms for the same logical maximum and confirmed subsequent durable replay. The full local regression file can pass while individual runs exceed the deadline. Host class affects margins; these samples do not isolate a specific Docker or GitHub scheduling cause. ## RCA-02 — Disjoint serializable requests

Of 83 real 40001 errors, **43 surfaced at COMMIT**, 22 while reconstructing results after audit (10 posting, seven audit, five journal reads), nine sealing the journal, five inserting postings, two inserting journals and two inserting audit. No observed failure surfaced at receipt lookup or reservation. A conflict reported at COMMIT may involve an earlier predicate read. Zero deadlocks were observed in this diagnostic. Width two had 20 serialization failures across 48 two-leg commands, all recovered within the existing three attempts. Width one had none across 48 two-leg commands. Three width-four requests exhausted retries across the two- and 50-leg workloads. Keep intermediate SQLSTATEs separate from failed logical requests.

The restricted writer’s fresh EXPLAIN selected the scoped unique receipt index, journal operation index, posting journal index and an indexed audit join. After history growth plus explicit ANALYZE, the receipt and operation probes used sequential scans, as did both sides of the audit join. The posting aggregate retained its bitmap index scan. Pre-COMMIT locks included a relation predicate on the initially sparse receipt index, index-page predicates, and later relation predicates on ledger_operation, journal, ledger_account and audit_event. PostgreSQL can conservatively track a broader predicate than one logical account or key. Relation/page predicates and the errors on disjoint accounts support this mechanism. An EXPLAIN of a standalone representative query and sampled locks do not identify the two edges of any individual SSI cycle, or the cached nested trigger plans. History, statistics, physical layout and time changed together. No case for a new index, forced planner setting or global serialization is established. [PostgreSQL serializable isolation](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/transaction-iso.html#XACT-SERIALIZABLE)

## Benchmark methodology and capacity

For 32 disjoint CORRECT50/c1 commands, fixture preparation (including 32 real original POSTs and 3,200 new accounts) took **3.043 s** and command execution took **4.104 s**. Database size grew from 11,272,719 to 15,221,263 bytes, postings from 1,632 to 6,432. First-response latency p50/p95 was 124.40/165.60 ms. Stage p50/p95 was 10.85/18.28 ms for posting inserts, 74.23/96.84 ms for COMMIT, 18.45/29.32 ms for live attestation, 0.04/0.16 ms for writer connection wait and 0.04/0.06 ms for verifier wait. Each measured request authenticated and attested once, with no retries. Internal retries repeat authentication and attestation in other cells. The artifact retains all individual stage timings and per-cell history. No clean monotonic history slowdown or pool bottleneck was established. Setup, index growth and repeated validation consume real resources. Extrapolating 32 traced observations to 1,000 would obscure changes in history and runner scheduling.

The historical batched critical campaign charged 1,000 original POSTs to the same 240 s budget, reached 574 measured commands (573 positive, one cancelled), and left 426 unmeasured. It did not record separate setup/execution durations, so the original split is unrecoverable. The 806-observation predecessor and 790-observation comparison baseline remain historical results. Historical and current CORRECT50 p99 remain **NOT PROVEN**. The proposed amendment predesignates POST2/c2 for qualified p99, retains representative CORRECT50, measures setup separately while charging it to the same cap, and requires independent positive maximum tests. ## Next technical decision

1. **Recommended next step for review: design a database-side elimination of redundant full-journal assertions before authorizing a prototype.** Explore scheduling the expensive validation at the irrevocable DRAFT → POSTED transition while preserving database-enforced validation of every supported mutation path. Keep per-leg identity, draft revision, archive/ownership checks and multiset validation. Model insert/update/delete of draft legs, historical drafts, moved legs, opening markers, same-transaction archive, direct SQL, legacy workflows and deferred execution before proposing trigger DDL. If even one path can commit invalid state, reject that design. An additive migration, private-schema function binding, invariant-catalog changes, clean/upgrade checks and lock-order analysis would all require review. This could remove the dominant repeated work at c1. It could also move lock acquisition or alter error timing, including near cancellation or COMMIT.
2. **SSI: retain the current runner and retry policy.** If c2 positive qualification fails, propose a paired diagnostic with identical seeded history and statistics, disjoint versus overlapping accounts, matched key distribution and equivalent writer-role plans. Collect per-attempt errors and safe predicate metadata without changing planner settings. Benefit is distinguishing a reproducible c2 reliability defect from a stress-only failure. It offers no immediate latency improvement. A global queue or speculative index would change lock/SSI behavior without the needed evidence.
3. **Regression contract for any approved prototype.** Require initial 201 on all three fresh max runs locally and in CI, exact multiset/amounts, one receipt/audit, two correctly scoped children and zero drafts. Run all S05–S06C invariants, direct-SQL negative and archive races, two-user restricted handlers, concurrent same/conflicting key, reverse/correct contention, real 40001/40P01, rollback at every boundary, cancellation before COMMIT, loss of COMMIT acknowledgement and fresh authorized immutable replay. Compare before/after at matched history and role with instrumented and uninstrumented trials reported separately. Measure insert and COMMIT independently, first-response failures, retries, scheduled/observed counts and cleanup. Preserve all existing timing budgets, limits and approved grants. Acceptance of the capacity policy alone does not authorize this experiment.
